## Q1. Top US apps per category and their revenue / subscriber scale (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Duolingo is the dominant revenue leader in US Education (#1 US iOS Education top-grossing in 2026 snapshots; FY2025 revenue $1.04B). Below it, the category splits three ways: (a) AI-native language tutors that have already reached $50–100M+ revenue (Speak, Praktika); (b) homework/Q&A apps where AI-native ByteDance Gauth leads downloads but monetization is weak; (c) course/skills platforms (Coursera+Udemy merged, MasterClass, Skillshare). Kids is led by brand/IP-driven subscription apps (ABCmouse, Lingokids, Toca Boca) whose revenue is mostly undisclosed. In Books, AI-heavy serialized audio (Pocket FM at $500M ARR) and book-summary apps (Headway, about $160M) are the fastest-growing, next to licensed-catalog giants (Audible, Kindle) for which this research found no fresh revenue disclosures.

### Inferences
- Revenue in US Education is very concentrated. Duolingo alone (~$1.2B in 2026) likely exceeds the combined consumer-app revenue of every other app named in this brief except the Coursera/Udemy combination, which is mostly web and enterprise rather than App Store.
- Test-prep and certification question-bank apps (CDL, EMS) rank high in Education grossing. Their product is a human-written question bank, which is a lesser-noticed but highly AI-substitutable content type.
- Books/audio has the clearest example of AI-produced content at scale making money: Pocket FM at $500M ARR. Kids has the weakest evidence of AI-native traction.

## Q2. Chegg's collapse and Duolingo's "AI-first" shift: concrete numbers

### Takeaway
Chegg is the clearest example of a human-expert Q&A business destroyed by generative AI. Subscribers fell ~31–40% YoY through 2025, it cut staff by 22% and then 45% in 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue was $46.5M (-40% YoY) as it winds down academic services. Duolingo is the opposite case, an incumbent that turned AI against its own content costs. It launched 148 AI-built courses in ~1 year versus 12 years for its first 100, phased out contractors, and kept ~72–73% gross margin, with AI inference cost (Video Call) now the main margin swing factor instead of human content labor.

### Inferences
- In Duolingo's P&L, the content cost that used to scale with human contractors (course writers, translators) has largely been replaced by inference cost (Video Call, Explain My Answer). Its gross margin moves on per-unit AI cost, not headcount. Per-unit AI cost is falling (73.0% in Q1 2026), so the self-disruptor is not under margin pressure.
- Chegg's model of paid human experts writing step-by-step answers, monetized via SEO traffic, was hit twice: AI generates the answer for free, and Google AI Overviews and chatbots capture the traffic. The ~$46.5M quarterly run-rate in Q3 2026 is a fraction of its earlier scale. Its 2022 peak revenue was not retrieved in this session.
- Duolingo's sharp deceleration in 2026 (14% revenue growth vs 39%) means part of the AI tailwind is now competitive pressure, from AI-native tutors (Speak, Praktika) and from general chatbots that can tutor languages. Evidence directly attributing the slowdown to them was not found.

## Q5. Barriers to AI-native replacement

### Takeaway
Barriers are highest in Kids (COPPA amendments enforceable since April 22, 2026, plus parental trust and licensed IP) and in licensed Books (content rights). They are lowest in homework/Q&A, where they have already been breached, and in serialized audio fiction. In language and skills learning, the main barriers are brand, habit/gamification scale (Duolingo at 64.9M DAU) and credentials (Coursera university certificates), not regulation.

### Cited Findings
- **COPPA**: the FTC published the amended COPPA Rule on April 22, 2025, effective June 23, 2025, with full compliance required by April 22, 2026. Operators need separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's personal information to third parties for targeted advertising, AI training, or other non-integral purposes. The FTC has signaled enforcement as a priority. — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule); [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html); [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule)

## Q6. Human-content cost exposure and per-category verdict: where can a cheaper AI-native replacement win?

### Takeaway
Ranked by how much of the incumbent's value was human-produced content that AI now makes cheaply, and how much room is left for a new entrant:
1. **Homework/Q&A (Chegg, Course Hero, Brainly)**: fully disrupted, but captured by general AI, so there is little room for a new standalone app.
2. **Serialized audio and summaries (Pocket FM-style audio fiction, Headway/Blinkist)**: AI-first models are already winning at $160–500M scale; a strong but crowded opportunity.
3. **Language learning**: incumbents split between self-disruptor Duolingo and exposed human-heavy players (Babbel Live already shut, Rosetta Stone, Busuu). AI-native tutors have proven $50–100M+ outcomes, so new entrants need a niche.

6. **Licensed books (Audible, Kindle, Libby)**: rights-gated, and the incumbents are themselves adopting AI narration and translation. Low opportunity for replacement and high opportunity for tooling.

Gauth-scale downloads have not produced large revenue | **Disrupted; value went to general AI. Avoid head-on; consider school-sold, curriculum-aligned tutoring** |
| Education – Language (Duolingo, Babbel, Busuu, Rosetta Stone) | High historically (course writers, translators, live tutors) | Duolingo self-disrupted (148 AI courses); Babbel Live shut; Speak/Praktika at $50–100M+ | Medium: speaking/conversation is now crowded. White space is in vertical niches (exam English such as IELTS/TOEFL, workplace English, heritage languages, older learners) | **Proven AI-native wins; incumbent Duolingo is strong. Niche entry only** |
| Education – Test prep / certification (CDL, EMS, nursing, etc.) | High: human-written question banks and explanations | Little evidence of AI disruption found | Medium-high: high willingness to pay, top-grossing; AI can generate item banks, but accuracy and exam alignment are gating | **Under-explored opportunity (needs validation)** |
| Education – Courses/skills (Udemy, Skillshare, MasterClass, Coursera) | Very high: instructor video production | Traffic loss; defensive Coursera–Udemy merger; MasterClass adding AI topics | Medium-high: AI-generated, personalized, interactive upskilling; but credentials (Coursera) and celebrity (MasterClass) are moats | **Instructor-marketplace layer (Udemy/Skillshare-type) most exposed** |
| Education – Music (Simply Piano, Yousician) | Medium: arrangements/lessons, but value is in feedback tech and song licensing | No major AI-native challenger found | Low-medium | **Not primarily a content-cost play** |
| Kids (ABCmouse, Lingokids, Toca Boca, PBS Kids, Khan Kids, Epic!, Homer, Prodigy) | High: curriculum, animation, illustration, licensed characters | AI story apps sub-scale; incumbents shifting to licensed IP | Low near-term: COPPA (Apr 2026), parental trust, Kids Category rules, free nonprofits (PBS, Khan Kids) | **Weakest replacement case; best wedge is parent-facing (personalized reading/speech tutor) with compliant architecture** |
| Books – Licensed catalog (Audible, Kindle, Libby) | Medium-high: human narration, translation | Incumbents adopting AI narration/translation themselves | Low for replacement (rights); high for tooling and long-tail self-pub | **Rights moat holds; AI benefits incumbents** |
| Books – Serialized audio fiction (Pocket FM, Kuku FM) | Was high (voice actors, writers) | Already AI-native (93% AI catalog) at $500M ARR | Medium: proven model, but UA costs dominate and Pocket FM is entrenched | **AI-native already won; competition shifts to distribution** |
| Books – Summaries/microlearning (Blinkist, Headway, Imprint) | High: summary writers, narrators | Headway at ~$160M using AI and experimentation; general chatbots can summarize any book | Medium: commoditized by chatbots; winners compete on habit, UX and paid acquisition | **Content cost no longer the moat; growth marketing is** |
| Reference (dictionary, translation, Bible) | Varies | No data gathered | — | **Insufficient evidence (see Gaps)** |

- Cross-category pattern: once AI makes content cheap, the binding constraints move to (1) distribution and paid acquisition (Kuku FM marketing > revenue; Headway's experimentation engine), (2) rights and IP (Audible catalog, Lingokids' Disney/Blippi), and (3) trust and regulation (COPPA). An AI-native challenger needs an advantage on at least one of these, not just a cheaper content cost.
- Self-disrupting incumbents (Duolingo, Audible, Khan Academy) have been more successful than pure-play replacements in their categories, except where the incumbent's core asset was the human answer itself (Chegg).

### Gaps
- No quantitative split of incumbents' COGS between human content production and other costs was found for any company (Chegg expert payments, Babbel content team, ABCmouse curriculum and animation, Audible narrator fees, Udemy instructor revenue share). The verdicts on content-cost dependence are qualitative.
- Reference apps (Merriam-Webster, Google Translate, DeepL, YouVersion Bible), Wattpad, Webtoon, Libby, Imprint and YouTube Kids were not researched because the search budget ran out.
- No Sensor Tower/Appfigures data on how many AI-native apps sit in the US iOS top 100 grossing for Education, Kids or Books in 2026.
