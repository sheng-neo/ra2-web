## Key Question 1: Who are the top US apps in each category, how much do they earn (2025–2026), and what is the human-labor component?

### Takeaway
The revenue leaders are incumbents and platforms that have already added AI: ChatGPT in Productivity, CapCut and Canva in Photo & Video and Design, Intuit and H&R Block in tax, Adobe in creative software. They are growing. The human-labor-heavy models that are still growing (TurboTax Live, LegalZoom subscriptions) market themselves as "human-in-the-loop." The ones visibly shrinking are the human-curation model (Stitch Fix) and first-wave single-trick AI photo apps (Remini, plus Splice).

  - Canva US mobile: about $3M per week ≈ **$150–170M per year**.

| App / segment | Human labor sold or embedded | AI exposure |
|---|---|---|

## Key Question 2: What are the concrete labor-replacement economics (human vs. incumbent hybrid vs. AI)?

### Takeaway
Across every human service in scope, AI cuts price by roughly 75–99%:
- Transcription: about 8x cheaper.
- Translation: about 3–40x cheaper.
- Headshots: 75–90% cheaper.
- Investment management: 75% cheaper (robo vs. human, a decade-old shift).
- Tax: Intuit already undercuts independent human preparers with AI-assisted experts.

The remaining human premium attaches to *signature, liability and trust*, not to labor hours.

### Cited Findings

- NATP 2025 Fee Study, base Form 1040 with Schedules 1–3: **CPAs $280, EAs $228, non-credentialed preparers about $185**. — [NATP 2025 Fee Study media highlights (PDF)](https://dam.natptax.com/m/5f112786acbd1130/original/2025_NATP_Fee_Study_MediaHighlights.pdf). ## Key Question 4: What barriers stop a cheaper AI-native alternative (regulation, data access, incumbent bundling, App Store rules)?

### Takeaway
The binding constraints differ by category:
- Finance: data-access cost (JPMorgan now charges aggregators) and SEC/FTC claims scrutiny.
- Tax: IRS/state e-file authorization plus liability. Instead shows authorization is achievable in about a year.
- Legal: state unauthorized-practice-of-law (UPL) rules plus FTC substantiation (DoNotPay).
- Photo, productivity and utilities: Apple/Google bundling and Apple's 4.3 anti-spam enforcement.

### Cited Findings

**Legal / FTC**
- FTC vs DoNotPay ("world's first robot lawyer"):
  - Complaint Sept 2024: the service was not tested to perform like a human lawyer, and the company did not retain attorneys to check it.
  - Final order approved 5–0 on 2025-01-16 (press release Feb 2025). DoNotPay pays $193,000, must notify 2021–2023 subscribers, and is barred from claiming its service performs like a real lawyer without substantiating evidence.
  — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/87474); [CO/AI summary](https://about.getcoai.com/news/ftc-bans-donotpays-ai-lawyer-claims-and-orders-refunds)
- UPL reform:
  - Arizona chose an ABS-only model (non-lawyer ownership allowed, UPL kept) and grew from 19 to 136 entities.
  - Utah's sandbox (which waives UPL, so software can provide legal services under oversight) shrank from 39 to 11 entities.
  - In Arizona, two ABS entities and their compliance lawyers faced discipline.
  — [Stanford CLP via LawNext (June 2025)](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/06/five-years-after-reform-stanford-study-offers-comprehensive-look-at-legal-innovation-in-arizona-and-utah.html)
- Utah's regulatory-reform committee is discussing AI-specific UPL carve-outs, for example with disclaimers (minutes from Nov–Dec 2025 and 2026). — [Utah Courts minutes 2025-12-19](https://www.utcourts.gov/content/dam/committees/regulatory_reform/minutes/2025.12.19%20AI_Legal%20Tech%20Approved%20Minutes.pdf)
- A TRI/NCSC white paper (Aug 2025) calls the UPL patchwork "a significant barrier" to AI legal tools. — [NCSC AI UPL white paper](https://www.ncsc.org/sites/default/files/media/document/AI_UPL_WhitePaper.pdf)

**Investment advice / SEC**
- First "AI-washing" cases (2024-03-18): Delphia (a robo-adviser that claimed AI use it did not have) paid $225K. Global Predictions ("first regulated AI financial advisor") paid $175K. — [O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/sec-targets-ai-washing-in-charges-against-two-investment-advisers); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2024/04/securities-and-exchange-commission-brings-first-enforcement-actions-over-aiwashing)

**Bank data access (Plaid / CFPB 1033)**
- CFPB finalized the Section 1033 open-banking rule in Oct 2024, with the first compliance dates for the largest banks around July 2026. A federal court in Kentucky has blocked enforcement while the CFPB reconsiders. An Aug 2025 ANPR explicitly reopened the question of fees. — [PYMNTS 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/bank-regulation/2026/data-aggregators-push-secure-access-as-rule-1033-rewrite-looms/); [BPI comment letter Oct 2025](https://bpi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/BPI-comment-re-CFPB-1033-ANPR-10-21-25.pdf)
- JPMorgan began charging aggregators for data access. It cited more than 2B data requests, over 90% of them not tied to a direct customer request. It reached a paid deal with Plaid (Sept 2025) at a reported "fractions of a cent per pull". Plaid said it would not pass the fees to its about 7,000 clients. — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/jpmorganchase-reaches-deal-to-charge-plaid-for-customer-data); [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fintechs-blast-jpmorgan-over-data-fees-open-banking-dimon-plaid-aggregators-block-mx-dave/753087/)

**Tax / IRS**
- IRS Direct File was cancelled for 2026 ("No launch date has been set for the future"). This removes a free government competitor. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/a-free-tax-filing-option-just-disappeared)
- A new entrant needs government e-file testing and approval per jurisdiction. Instead reached 100% of jurisdictions in about 2 months of disclosed progress (67% on 3/2 → 100% on 5/8/2026). Final sign-off on returns is still described as done by a qualified accountant for B2B agents. — [Instead](https://instead.com/news/instead-first-new-tax-platform-100-percent-us-efile-approvals-all-jurisdictions); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/rawveg/when-ai-files-your-taxes-5cfp)

**Incumbent / OS bundling**
- iOS 26 (Sept 2025) adds a **Preview app on iPhone** for viewing and editing PDFs with AutoFill, a document scanner and export. It also adds **Live Translation** in Messages, FaceTime and Phone, plus AirPods Pro 3 in-person translation and Live Voicemail transcripts. — [Apple iOS 26 All New Features PDF](https://www.apple.com/os/pdf/All_New_Features_iOS_26_Sept_2025.pdf); [MacRumors iOS 26 release notes](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/09/ios-26-release-notes/)
- Amazon built Rufus into its own app (250M users, $10B incremental sales). Standalone AI shopping assistants compete against a free, first-party one. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/02/amazon-rufus-ai-shopping-assistant-chatbot-10-billion-sales-monetization/)
- ChatGPT dominates both Productivity grossing and the overall US grossing chart, which makes general-purpose "AI wrapper" productivity apps a direct substitute target. — [App Radar](https://appradar.com/blog/top-grossing-apps-app-store)

**App Store review**
- Apple reportedly "does not have any rules specifically against vibe coding apps". In March 2026 it blocked updates for vibe-coding apps (Replit, Vibecode) over code generated and executed after review. Guideline 4.3 (Spam) now says low-effort apps in oversaturated categories may be removed if not updated or not attracting customers. — [MacRumors App Review Guidelines roundup](https://www.macrumors.com/roundup/app-store-review-guidelines/); [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/apple-app-store-spam-guidelines/); [Apple Developer Forums 4.3(a) thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/780460)

### Inferences
- **Legal** is the most regulation-constrained category. A consumer "AI lawyer" must avoid claiming lawyer-equivalence (FTC) and avoid individualized legal advice outside Utah or Arizona-style structures. A viable design is an AI front end plus a licensed-attorney network, which is what LegalZoom already sells as "human-in-the-loop".
- **Tax:** the barrier is surmountable. Instead got all-jurisdiction e-file approval. The harder moats are trust and liability (audit defense) and Intuit's customer acquisition. Direct File's end slightly *helps* challengers by removing the free public option.
- **Finance:** JPMorgan's fees and the uncertain 1033 rule raise the unit cost of bank-connected AI budgeting and coaching. They hurt small entrants more than Intuit/Credit Karma or Cleo at scale. SEC AI-washing precedent means "AI financial advisor" marketing needs registered-adviser compliance.
- **Photo, scanner, translator, transcription and notes:** the main barrier is not regulation but **zero-price OS features** (Preview scanner, Live Translation, Apple Intelligence) plus ChatGPT and Gemini as universal substitutes. Apple's 4.3 enforcement makes "me-too" wrappers in saturated categories (scanner, cleaner, AI photo filter) riskier to ship and keep listed.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Apple Intelligence "Clean Up" in Photos or iOS 18.1 call recording with transcription in Notes. I believe both exist, but this is unverified in this session.
- FINRA rules on AI chatbots for broker-dealers were not researched.
- I did not find specific state-bar ethics opinions on consumer AI legal apps.
- No current docket status for the 1033 litigation as of Nov 2026 was found.

### Inferences
- **Where AI-native can win (ranked by evidence strength):**
  1. **AI money coach with a credit or cash-advance engine.** Cleo and Dave show >$250M–550M revenue at profit. The model is proven, but it is lending economics, not software, and is exposed to bank data fees.
  2. **"Photographer replacement" for specific jobs** (professional headshots, e-commerce and marketplace listing photos, real-estate, retouching). Price is 75–90% below humans, and bootstrapped teams reached $3–10M ARR. Risk: the feature gets absorbed by Canva, PhotoRoom and the marketplaces themselves (Poshmark, ThredUp).
  3. **AI-prepared, human-signed tax filing** at about $50–100 for returns where humans charge $185–280. E-file approval is achievable (Instead). The fight is against Intuit's $2.8B Live business and its DIY price cuts (FY27 guidance). A US consumer app could position as "CPA-quality for TurboTax-DIY price".
  4. **Vertical AI scribes and note-takers** (Otter and Plaud patterns) where a human scribe or assistant was paid. Generic meeting notes face Apple, Google and Microsoft bundling.
  5. **Legal:** AI document drafting plus a subscription attorney network, positioned clearly as "not a lawyer" (DoNotPay lesson). It competes with LegalZoom's $266 ARPU subscription.
- **Where AI-native will likely lose:**
  - Generic photo and video editing (CapCut, Canva, Meitu and Hypic subsidize AI).
  - Template marketplaces (gen-AI makes templates abundant, but Canva owns workflow and distribution).
  - Scanners, fax, translators and transcription-only apps (free OS features).
  - Pure budgeting subscriptions (falling app revenue, data-access costs).
  - Stand-alone AI shopping assistants (Rufus and platform-native assistants).
  - One-trick viral AI photo effects (Remini decline; Lightricks restructuring).
- **Pattern for this report:** AI does not remove the human in the apps that make the most money. It lets the incumbent sell "AI + expert" at a lower cost per case (TurboTax Live +37%, H&R Block AI Tax Assist, LegalZoom human-in-the-loop). A challenger therefore needs either (a) a regulated wrapper of its own (e-file authorization, an RIA, an attorney network), or (b) a job where no license is needed and the human price anchor is high (photography, scribing, translation of non-certified content).

### Gaps
- No quantitative churn or retention comparison was found between AI-native photo apps and incumbents.
- No US top-grossing rank data was found for AI-native tax or legal consumer apps. They appear to be B2B or web-first so far.
- Shopping verdict evidence is thin: no data on Temu, Shein, price-comparison or AI-stylist app revenue.
- Utilities verdict lacks VPN and keyboard revenue data.
